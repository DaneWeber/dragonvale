After the Standing Stone, the players were too low of a level for the Heart of Nightfang Spire, so I included several hooks and the players essentially completed the _Against the Giants_ old adventure, using the original material but the updated 3e maps provided by WotC.

My current hypothesized path forward is to tackle the _Heart of Nightfang Spire_, head into the Underdark to deal with the Drow in Erelhei-Cinlu, and cap with the _Bastion of Broken Souls_. I've also included an option for _The Great Modron March_, if they're curious.

While reading various reviews and commentary, there's a bit about Nightfang Spire that I was tempted to change dramatically, although I believe I'm back to running it almost as written, but with the following changes in order to make me happy about internal consistency.

## Changes to the Overall Adventure

### Gulthias Motivation and Scheme

The books seems to indicate that Gulthias just wants to create a horde of undead and rule the nearby lands.

Reacting to the conclusion of https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52666/roleplaying-games/ex-rpgnet-review-heart-of-nightfang-spire I want more of a scheme.

Gulthias has Ashardalon's necromantic heart and his experimentation with flesh is intended to create a new body for Ashardalon so that he can return to the world.

In my cosmology (closely aligned with published 3e/3.5e material), I've elaborated the plane of shadow as shaped by thought, emotion, and memory. In this world, "losing your shadow" is about losing your memory and identity. When dying and crossing the river Styx, the soul is stripped of its shadow. The shadow plane is also the home of your mirror self, which is one form of your shadow.

Vampires have lost their soul, but are animated by negative energy and their shadow. This is why they cast no shadow and have no reflection.

A shadow of Ashardalon exists in the shadow plane around Nightfang Spire.

Gulthias' plan is to create a body for Ashardalon using the heart and binding Ashardalon's shadow to it.

### Gulthias Tree Connection to Ravenloft

As per https://scholarsandserpents.wordpress.com/2018/02/21/ashardalon-and-the-vampire-gulthias/ I like the idea that Gulthias traveled to a domain of dread, via the shadow, in order to learn the secrets required to bring Ashardalon back to life/unlife. That informs some of my changes. To this end, I am adding a (psionic) mind flayer who was the source of the information on how to travel to the Domains of Dread.

### Girallons to Quaggoths

Some folks have talked about Girallons going from Medium to Large in the change from 3e to 3.5e. While that's a relatively minor thing, it triggered me to replace them entirely with Quaggoths, since I really don't have a place for Girallons in my campaign world and I'm already planning to include Quaggoths in the Underdark. They're Medium, have low but non-animal intelligence, and have a good CR fit if using the Greater Quaggoth from page 138 of _The Drow of the Underdark_. I also hope the mix of the white-furred savage "beast-following" Quaggoths and stained-furred elevated "magic-following" Quaggoths will provide an avenue for players to realize that they should talk with Thiff instead of killing her immediately. 

### The Dragon Key

While a little silly and video-game-like, let's add the following properties to the Dragon Key:

- The parts of the Dragon Key must be present within the tower. If any part is removed more than 100ft from the tower, an alarm will sound from the core. A day later, the core will open.
- The parts of the Dragon Key cannot be brought within the core. Being placed within the core has the same effect as removing them from the tower.
- The parts of the Dragon Key emit a piercing sound when removed from the tower, audible with a Listen check of DC -20.

The flimsy rationale: the core was created prior to Gulthias planning to become a vampire. At the time, the thought was that trusted cultists might have to unlock the core if Gulthias were either away or incapacitated within. He now regrets the design, but it will take time and effort and power to change it.

## Creature Stats (3.5e)

### Quaggoth

Stats are from _The Drow of the Underdark_, pages 136-139. I plan to remove the magic items in favor of the published adventure, although that may require a little math.

#### Quaggoth (Raging)

```yaml
Quaggoth (Raging):
  CR: 3
  Type: Usually NE Medium monstrous humanoid
  Init: +4
  Senses: darkvision 120 ft., scent; Listen +3, Spot +2
  Languages: Undercommon
  AC: 13, touch 8, flat-footed 13 (+5 natural, –2 rage)
  hp: 34 (4d8 HD); Diehard
  Immune: fear, poison
  Saves: Fort +5, Ref +4, Will +7
  Weakness: light sensitivity
  Speed: 30 ft. (6 squares), climb 30 ft.
  Melee: 2 claws +10 each (1d4+6) and bite +5 (1d6+3)
  Space: 5 ft.
  Reach: 5 ft.
  Base Atk: +4
  Grp: +10
  Atk Options: Power Attack, quaggoth rage 1/day (7 rounds)
  Abilities: Str 22, Dex 11, Con 19, Int 7, Wis 12, Cha 10
  Feats: DiehardB, Improved Initiative, Power Attack
  Skills: Climb +14, Listen +3, Spot +2
  Advancement: by character class; Favored Class barbarian; see text
```

Light Sensitivity (Ex) A quaggoth is dazzled in bright sunlight or within the radius of a daylight spell.

Rage (Ex) Once per day, a quaggoth can fly into a screaming blood frenzy. This ability functions like a barbarian’s rage (PH 25), except that the quaggoth suffers no ill effects after the rage ends.

Skills Quaggoths have a +8 racial bonus on Climb checks. A quaggoth can always choose to take 10 on a Climb check, even if rushed or threatened.

*In areas of shadow, a quaggoth has a +4 circumstance bonus on Hide checks.

When not raging, a quaggoth has the following changed statistics:

```yaml
Grp: +8

```
